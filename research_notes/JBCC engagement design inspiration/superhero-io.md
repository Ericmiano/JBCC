## Which engagement patterns can inform JBCC without copying the game?

### Takeaway
There are no directly observed website patterns to transfer from `superhero.io`. The official product listing does document a game loop built on player choice, visible progression, and optional tasks; at most, these support a restrained recommendation to give visitors clear, user-controlled routes through JBCC’s information.

### Cited Findings
- The [developer’s Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.lorastudio.superhero2) describes leveling, a roster of characters, tasks, rewards, modes, and power-ups. These are claims about the game, not a live website.
- The listing describes the sequel as having a new UI and a larger map, but gives no case study or detailed interaction specification. [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.lorastudio.superhero2)
- W3C guidance explains that interaction-triggered, non-essential motion should be disableable under WCAG 2.3.3, and that some users experience distraction or nausea from motion. [W3C: Animation from Interactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions)
- W3C’s pause/stop/hide guidance covers automatically moving or updating content displayed alongside other content. [W3C: Pause, Stop, Hide](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide)

### Inferences
- Give the homepage hero one useful choice that immediately changes the next destination or its supporting information. Keep ordinary links visible so the interaction does not trap keyboard, screen-reader, or touch users.
- Replace “leveling” with real visitor progress: make it easy to tell what document is current, what seminar is next, what information is required, and what happens after contacting the Secretariat. These are recommendations for JBCC, not features observed on the reference.
- Use small, purposeful transitions on selection or entry, not continuous motion. Keep text readable before and after the transition; respect reduced-motion settings. If any non-essential motion starts automatically and persists, provide the applicable user control.
- For phone use and lower-bandwidth conditions, favor semantic HTML controls, compact locally served imagery, and CSS-scale transitions. Avoid making a WebGL/canvas scene, autoplay video, or external game embed a prerequisite for reaching JBCC’s core information.
- Preserve JBCC’s professional identity: real construction photography, contracts, indices, seminars, member representation, and dated source material are better engagement cues than superheroes, loot, ranks, combat, or game-like reward language.

### Gaps
- Because the exact site could not be inspected, there are no verified claims about its responsive layout, input methods, animation timing, mobile performance, or whether it offers reduced-motion handling.
- The app listing does not establish whether its gameplay features are suitable for a public-sector or professional organization website; the transfer suggestions above are design judgment only.

## What should JBCC avoid, and are there official explanations of the reference?

### Takeaway
Do not copy the game’s identity or treat its product loop as a proven institutional-site pattern. The only current primary source found is a store listing; it documents a game and support link, not the design intent or implementation of a `superhero.io` website.

### Cited Findings
- The [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.lorastudio.superhero2) marks the game as containing ads and in-app purchases and promotes daily tasks, rewards, a luck spin, and character unlocking.
- The official listing’s “public Website” link points to [tokgame.cn](https://tokgame.cn/), while the requested [superhero.io](https://superhero.io/) page could not be fetched. This mismatch is why the exact reference remains ambiguous.
- W3C states that non-essential motion animation triggered by interaction should be disableable at WCAG 2.3.3 Level AAA; its rationale discusses distraction and motion sickness. [W3C: Animation from Interactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions)
- W3C’s WCAG 2.2 guidance says qualifying automatically moving, blinking, or scrolling content presented alongside other content needs a pause, stop, or hide mechanism. [W3C: Pause, Stop, Hide](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide)

### Inferences
- Avoid rankings, streaks, random spins, unlock gates, ad-like promotional interruptions, constant leaderboard motion, and rewards that make official documents feel like game prizes. These would undermine the seriousness and predictability expected for contracts and professional guidance.
- Avoid using famous superhero characters, game artwork, logos, or other protected identity elements as JBCC art direction. The institutional site should use licensed JBCC/construction-sector media.
- Avoid parallax, cursor-following effects, rapid scene changes, flashing, and animation that carries essential text. Keep motion optional, brief, keyboard/touch operable, and secondary to direct navigation.
- A heavy interactive 3D or game embed would add a needless loading and bandwidth dependency to a site whose primary jobs are finding official documents and contacting the council.

### Gaps
- I found no official case study, design-system documentation, source repository, or developer explanation tied to the requested website.
- The Google Play listing does not explain the game’s UI implementation or accessibility. It should be treated as a primary source for published product features only, not for the usability or technical qualities of the live site.
