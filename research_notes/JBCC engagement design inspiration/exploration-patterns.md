# Engaging interaction patterns for JBCC

Research date: 8 October 2026. This note records observable patterns from live, official institutional sites and separates those facts from proposed adaptations. The “effort” labels below are estimates for a small institutional website, not measured vendor estimates.

## Which interactions encourage visitors to explore beyond the hero?

### Takeaway

The strongest patterns give visitors a useful next action and a reason to follow related material: choose a need, refine a collection, follow a story, or move between records and their context. They invite exploration through content and utility; motion is an accent rather than the attraction.

### Cited Findings

- **Need-based pathway (low–medium effort).** AAK’s live homepage has three direct actions—join, view events, and validate a certificate—and a “Find your fit” selector. The selector asks for a professional discipline and career stage, then says it will show the relevant chapter and membership fee. This is a concrete two-step path for a complex organization with several audiences. [AAK homepage](https://aak.or.ke/)
- **A map that exposes real records (medium–high effort).** The Aga Khan Award for Architecture Project Finder asks visitors to search projects by area, year, or building type. UNESCO’s Urban Heritage Atlas describes a more layered implementation: visitors move among city scales and heritage attributes, expand a legend, and select map features for explanatory pop-ups. The Atlas includes African cases such as Asmara and Djenné; its index labels Lamu as “coming soon.” [Aga Khan Award Project Finder](https://the.akdn/en/how-we-work/our-agencies/aga-khan-trust-culture/aga-khan-award-for-architecture/aga-khan-award-for-architecture-project-finder), [UNESCO Atlas overview](https://whc.unesco.org/en/urban-heritage-atlas/), [UNESCO Asmara case](https://whc.unesco.org/en/urban-heritage-atlas/asmara)
- **An editorial route through a collection (medium effort).** RIBA says it curated 15 Google Arts & Culture stories from collection photography, drawings, models, audio, and video. The Library of Congress describes its Story Maps as collection stories combining narrative, multimedia, and interactive maps, including a map story about prayer traditions in Africa, the Middle East, and Central Asia. [RIBA and Google Arts & Culture](https://www.architecture.com/explore-architecture/google-arts-and-culture), [Library of Congress Story Maps](https://www.loc.gov/research-centers/african-and-middle-eastern/researcher-resources/story-maps/)
- **A time-based spatial story (higher effort).** RIBA’s exhibition page describes Liverpool’s Digital City Model as an interactive 3D model used to tell stories about the city’s past, present, and future. This is a strong example of navigation through a real place and its change over time, but requires substantial verified spatial data and is not a necessary pattern for JBCC. [RIBA exhibitions](https://www.old.architecture.com/explore-architecture/exhibitions)

### Inferences

- Put three or four task choices immediately below or within the hero: “Find a contract,” “Check an index,” “Explore a seminar,” and “Ask the Council.” Each should open a short, factual preview with the next link. This lets visitors continue into the site without requiring a full map or a decorative hero animation.
- If JBCC has authoritative county- or project-level records, a map can make those records browsable. A useful first version could be a small county index with a selectable map and the same information in a list. Do not invent a project or seminar map if the underlying records do not carry verified locations.
- Treat a timeline or city model as a later editorial project, not a homepage default. The richer example is successful because a real, curated corpus supports it; an empty or approximate visualization would reduce trust.
- Short transitions can confirm that a choice was made, but do not auto-rotate the visitor’s selection or hijack the scroll. Keep the next action visible after the interaction.

### Gaps

- These sources document features and institutional intent, not analytics showing that a given feature increased return visits, reading depth, or document use. The proposed engagement benefits are design inferences, not measured outcomes.
- The AKAA Finder page exposes search criteria in its accessible page text, but its result-selection behavior was not inspectable in the text-only browser view. Avoid assuming that its map or visual transitions are fully keyboard- or screen-reader-accessible.

## What works for an archive or professional body with real documents, projects, and constituent groups?

### Takeaway

For JBCC, the most adaptable examples are the searchable professional register, category-based resource centre, and archives that connect individual documents to places, people, and explanations. The common pattern is useful metadata plus multiple routes into the same primary record.

### Cited Findings

- **Faceted resource centre (low–medium effort).** AAK’s Resource Centre groups reports, downloads, advocacy documents, building regulations, policy submissions, magazines, and event reports into labelled categories, with latest downloads shown separately. Its Status of the Built Environment Report archive identifies the years it covers. [AAK Resource Centre](https://aak.or.ke/resources)
- **Search with narrowing filters and alternate views (medium effort).** The Smithsonian Collections Search Center identifies search filters for online media, IIIF-enabled items, CC0 media, repository/category, and broad subject areas. Its tutorial explains further narrowing by record type and date, and its help material describes list/grid views, active filter chips, and clearing filters. [Smithsonian Collections Search Center](https://collections.si.edu/search/), [Smithsonian search tutorial](https://collections.si.edu/search/tutorial.htm), [National Museum of Asian Art search guide](https://asia-archive.si.edu/search-help-guide/)
- **A searchable professional register with verifiable status (medium effort).** Kenya’s BORAQS public register offers lookup by name, registration number, or certificate serial number and shows active status. AAK’s member directory adds filters by name/number, chapter, alphabet, and advanced criteria, while its certificate-verification page describes a public serial-number check. [BORAQS public register](https://boraqs.go.ke/registered/), [AAK member directory](https://members.aak.or.ke/directory), [AAK certificate verification](https://aak.or.ke/certificate-verification)
- **Records connected to context, not just a file list (medium–high effort).** The Accra Architecture Archive presents digitized archival records and visual material, including a 1906 building-permit application and site plan and a 1924 building regulation; its homepage connects the digital archive to exhibitions and “hi/stories” about the people and city. [Accra Archive homepage](https://www.accraarchive.com/), [Accra digital archive](https://www.accraarchive.com/archive)
- **A linked-data collection with more than one discovery route (high effort).** Digital Benin connects a searchable catalogue, institutions, provenance information, a map, oral histories, historical narratives, and archival documents. The platform says its catalogue brings together 5,304 objects from 139 institutions in 21 countries. Its visual research tools include a network explorer for people/institutions/companies and a “Paper Trails” view tracing objects through archival records and photographs. [Digital Benin](https://digitalbenin.org/), [Digital Benin research tools](https://digitalbenin.org/archive/visual-research-tools)
- **Map plus narrative and source disclosure.** UNESCO’s Atlas places a selectable map alongside written descriptions, historic imagery, and research context; its overview says each case is built from research, reflection, analysis, maps, plans, drawings, and photographs. The Atlas explicitly notes that its content is an educational resource and does not substitute for official UNESCO World Heritage statutory data. [UNESCO Urban Heritage Atlas](https://whc.unesco.org/en/urban-heritage-atlas/), [UNESCO Paris case and map](https://whc.unesco.org/en/urban-heritage-atlas/Paris)

### Inferences

- Organize JBCC resources by the questions visitors ask, then let them filter by document type, year/edition, and status. Keep a plain, keyboard-friendly list view. For each item, display its title, edition/date, source, file type, and currentness; label missing, historic, or superseded items explicitly rather than hiding them.
- A document detail view should connect the official file to its related documents and practical explanation: agreement form → relevant schedule/annex → advisory note → seminar or guidance. Offer a direct download at every stage. This adapts Accra Archive and Digital Benin’s record-to-context links without pretending JBCC has a museum-scale dataset.
- Borrow the trust mechanism from professional registers only where JBCC owns the underlying authority. A JBCC resource catalogue can show an approved edition and last-checked date; it should not imply that JBCC licenses professionals or verifies third-party records unless that is part of its mandate.
- Add filters only when the corpus supports them. With a modest starting catalogue, a clear categorized ledger with search may be more useful than a complex filter interface. Grow the facets as records acquire consistent metadata.
- Treat descriptions, source names, authorship, rights, issue dates, and version status as part of the interaction design. Missing metadata should be visibly marked and routed for editorial review rather than silently filled in.

### Gaps

- The public examples do not reveal JBCC’s rights to reproduce contract text, scanned documents, seminar recordings, or third-party images. Check permissions and copyright before building an annotated document viewer or visual exhibit.
- The Smithsonian and Digital Benin platforms operate at much larger collection scales than JBCC’s currently captured public documents. Their facet taxonomies and database architecture should not be copied wholesale.
- No primary-source evidence was found that a map is the best starting interaction for JBCC visitors. The map recommendation is conditional on the availability of meaningful, verified geographic data.

## How should motion and responsive behavior remain usable with keyboard, touch, reduced motion, and modest mobile connections?

### Takeaway

Make interaction user-directed and content-first. Keep the same actions available as text and links on every device, honor reduced-motion preferences, and load visual assets at the size and moment users need them.

### Cited Findings

- **Motion should be optional when it is non-essential.** W3C’s WCAG explanation for Success Criterion 2.3.3 says interaction-triggered animation should be disableable unless essential, describes possible dizziness/nausea/headache reactions, and names the reduced-motion preference as one technique. This criterion is Level AAA; it is useful design guidance, not a claim that every animation fails minimum WCAG conformance. [W3C: Animation from Interactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions)
- **Map content needs another access path.** GOV.UK map guidance recommends a text description, clear labels, data in a table or written summary, not relying on colour alone, and testing with assistive-technology users. Its policy-map guide also suggests alternative formats such as a static map. [GOV.UK map design guidance](https://brand.design-system.service.gov.uk/data/maps/), [GOV.UK presenting a policies map](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-to-present-your-policies-map)
- **Responsive imagery reduces wasted transfer.** web.dev recommends serving image candidates sized to the user’s viewport. It notes that serving desktop-sized images to mobile devices can use two to four times more data than needed. [web.dev: Serve responsive images](https://web.dev/articles/serve-responsive-images)
- **Autoplay video has a real transfer cost.** web.dev says preload="auto" downloads the full video whether or not the visitor presses play; it recommends playback controls for videos so users can stop, pause, and adjust volume. [web.dev: Video basics](https://web.dev/articles/video-basics)

### Inferences

- Keep the hero image as the default, with a small, cropped responsive image set. If JBCC has a meaningful video, make it an explicit play action with a still poster, captions, and controls; avoid a large, continuously looping background video.
- Use the shared reveal and image/line wrappers already specified in project instructions. Keep scroll reveals short, preserve the final visible state if a user changes their motion preference, and disable non-essential movement for reduced-motion users. Use GSAP only for a small, bounded transition where it makes a state change clearer; do not attach decorative animations to the map, document text, or core controls.
- Make visual selection controls real buttons or links with labels and visible focus. On touch, require no hover state; on keyboard, support arrow/tab behavior appropriate to the control. For map markers, add a text list with the same destinations and allow the list to operate as the primary control.
- Lazy-load off-screen images and interactive embeds, avoid loading a heavy map or video before it is requested, and set image dimensions to prevent content shift. These measures help conserve data and make page movement predictable.
- Use lightweight opacity or underline changes to signal a selection. Do not make parallax or zooming necessary to understand which resource was selected.

### Gaps

- No network profile or device-use data for JBCC visitors was available. The recommendation to prefer a still hero and defer video/map payloads is a conservative engineering inference, not a finding about JBCC’s measured traffic.
- Search results for “Superhero.io” did not resolve to one unambiguous official design reference, so this note does not attribute a particular observed pattern to that name. The comparisons above are from identifiable institutional sites and include multiple interactive examples beyond that reference.
